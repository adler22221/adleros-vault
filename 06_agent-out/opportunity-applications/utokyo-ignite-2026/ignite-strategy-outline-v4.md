## What the job posting actually says it wants

The posting lists five kinds of people it's looking for, and says explicitly that matching any
one or several of the five is enough — it isn't asking anyone to satisfy all five at once. In
full, paraphrased closely:

1. Someone who has done new-business support or accelerator work for companies or universities,
   but focuses on growth rather than revenue, and wants to accompany university students growing
   at high speed as a startup-mentor type.
2. Someone who can do the unglamorous parts of running a program — outreach, participant
   management, logistics, grading — while also thinking at a higher level about how the program
   itself should be designed and run more efficiently.
3. Someone who was an early member of a company, social venture, or internal venture, is still
   actively running that project or building it now, cares seriously about educating the next
   generation of innovators, and wants to do both at once on a half-commit basis — stabilizing
   half their annual income through this center.
4. Someone in their late twenties to early thirties who wants to build a team and keep doing this
   work for three years or longer.
5. Someone who eventually wants to become a UTokyo professor, whether they already hold a PhD or
   just intend to pursue one.

Nowhere in this list, or anywhere else in the posting, is there a request for evidence of a long
personal history living in or connecting to Japan. That's the fact that matters most for what
comes next.

## What actually went wrong in the earlier strategy work

Two documents from earlier in this process — one called the research report, one called the
strategist's fit-judgment and strategy-options write-up — took a fact the posting states neutrally
("the posting itself contains no explicit statement about foreign-national or language
eligibility") and quietly turned that neutral silence into a risk that needed defending against.
The research report named "a genuine, years-long Japan network — not a cold international
application" as one of only three things it called your strongest evidence for this specific
job. The strategy document that came after it inherited that framing and hardened it further,
specifically naming your JLPT N1 certificate, your research affiliation with Tekisen Democratic
University in Japan (2017–2019), and a conference paper you gave to the Japan Materialism
Research Association (唯物論研究協会, a small critical-theory academic society) as proof of a
decade-long Japan commitment.

None of that — JLPT N1 as an argument, Tekisen as a "throughline," the Materialism Research
Association at all — appears anywhere in your own words in [[20260807 UTOKYO IGNITE]]. And as
laid out above, the posting itself never asks for that kind of proof. The same mechanism
happened a second time, more broadly: those same two documents also built an "applicant pool
splits into two competing clusters" model to argue you're more differentiated than other
candidates, when the posting's own instruction — matching any one or several of its five points
is enough — never asked for differentiation against imagined competitors at all. And it happened
a third time, specifically around the field asking why you're interested in this posting: those
documents called the documentary you made at fourteen "the" answer to that question, and
prescribed the opening line as "seeing the posting on JREC-IN." Your actual answer, in your own
words, is different: a former mentee saw the posting and told you about it. Those documents
couldn't have known that, since you hadn't told me that part yet when they were written — but
now that you have, they need correcting, not carrying forward.
