## Why the last version was wrong, briefly

Two rounds of audit turned up the same failure in three places. `research-report.md` took a
neutral fact from the posting — it never says anything about foreign-national or language
eligibility — and quietly turned that silence into a risk to defend against, building "a
genuine, years-long Japan network, not a cold international application" into one of only three
things it called your strongest evidence. `strategy-options.md` inherited that and hardened it
further, naming your JLPT N1, your time at Tekisen Democratic University, and your paper at the
Japan Materialism Research Association (唯物論研究協会) as proof of a decade-long Japan
commitment. None of that is in your own words anywhere in [[20260807 UTOKYO IGNITE]], and the
posting itself never asks for that kind of proof — it asks about program-ops experience,
mentoring track record, a forward three-year commitment, and half-commit fit. The same
mechanism did it twice more: `fit-judgment.md` and `strategy-options.md` also invented a
"two-cluster applicant pool" model to justify differentiation claims, when the posting's own
header says any one or several of its five bullets is welcome — no ranking against imagined
competitors was ever asked for. And separately, the documentary you made at fourteen got called
"the" answer to why you're interested in this posting, with Q6 prescribed to open on "seeing the
posting on JREC-IN" — a discovery story that turns out not to be true. Your actual answer, in
your own words, is a referral from a former mentee. Neither strategy document could have known
that, since you hadn't told it that part yet.

None of this means the earlier research was fabricated — checked against your CV and the
posting, almost everything in it is accurate. It means the *emphasis* answered questions nobody
asked, and that emphasis is what needs correcting, not the underlying facts.

## What the posting is actually asking for

The IGNITE posting names five kinds of people it welcomes, and it explicitly says any one or
several is enough — it isn't asking anyone to satisfy all five. It wants someone who can run
workshops but also handle the unglamorous parts of a program — outreach, participant
management, logistics, grading — while still being able to think about how the program itself
should be designed and made more efficient. It wants someone with their own ongoing project who
wants to keep it running while committing half their time here. It wants someone in their late
twenties to early thirties building a multi-year team. And it welcomes people with a PhD or the
intent to get one, eventually aiming at a UTokyo faculty position. Nowhere does it ask for
evidence of a long personal history in Japan — that's not one of the five things it's screening
for.
