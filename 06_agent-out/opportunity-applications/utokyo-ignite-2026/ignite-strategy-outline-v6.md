**One-sentence claim:** Adler's interest in this posting closes a loop — a former mentee he once accompanied as a teenager is now working inside Japan's research world herself, and it was she who pointed him back toward a program built to do for its students what he once did for her.

**Ordered beats:**
1. Open on the referral itself: Joann Shen (沈潔伃), whom Adler mentored years earlier, now works with a UTokyo professor in 当事者研究 (research led by the people directly affected by an issue, rather than by outside experts studying them — the professor stays unnamed, per Adler's instruction) and sent him the IGNITE posting because she judged him a good fit given his track record mentoring young people toward social innovation. 2. Situate the referral in time: it arrived not long after Adler spent about a month in Japan, in July 2026, presenting at conferences and exploring social-innovation opportunities there — Japan was already active in his thinking when the posting reached him.
3. State plainly, and only at this level, why he was in a position to act on it: he had applied for a Japan working-holiday visa, for a health-related reason, named at that level and no further. This is deliberate, not a gap — naming the specific diagnosis risks the field reading as "I came for medical care and happened to find a job," which undercuts the actual answer to "what drew your interest" rather than supporting it. How much more detail belongs here, if any, is still Adler's call — the beat above states the minimum on purpose.
4. Connect the visa/trip facts to a standing intention, in Adler's own words: he wants to strengthen his grounding in Japan in research and social innovation, to strengthen the Japan/East-Asia footing of his ongoing collaborative work and social-innovation initiatives more broadly. State this without theory/organization branding — describe the collaborative work in plain terms (a cross-cultural community of practice he's building with peers across Taiwan, Hong Kong, Japan, and Korea) rather than naming it.
5. Close by tying the three threads together: the referral, the recent trip, and the pre-existing wish to deepen his Japan grounding lined up, which is why he applied when he did.

## Section 6: Q11 — 紹介者 (referrer, optional)

**Recommend leaving blank**, reversing the earlier instinct to name Joann Shen here. Checked against Japanese HR-glossary convention (two independent sources: keireki.net/rsdsyokai/ and keireki.net/rsdtjskrb/): 紹介者 conventionally means an *insider* referral — someone with an existing professional tie to the hiring organization itself (リファラル採用), with example phrasing consistently structured as "someone who works at your company." Joann has no connection to the IGNITE center or its hiring side — only to her own unrelated lab elsewhere at UTokyo. Naming her here would imply an insider connection that doesn't exist and could read as a misunderstanding of the field, not a strength. She still appears fully in Q6 and Q7 as the referral story — this field is asking something narrower and different from "who told you about this posting," and the honest answer to that narrower question is that no one connected to the program did.
