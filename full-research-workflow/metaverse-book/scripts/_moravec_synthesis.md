### 1. The p.117 passage and the substance question — decisive verdict

Verbatim, from *Mind Children* ch. 4 ("Grandfather Clause"), pattern-identity vs. body-identity, p.117 (via retrieved `cited_text`, OCR artifacts intact — "1 will call", "onlv"):

> "Pattern-identity, conversely, defines the essence of a person, say myself, as the pattern and the process going on in my head and body, not the machinery supporting that process. If the process is preserved, 1 am preserved. **The rest is mere jelly.** The body-identity position, 1 think, is based on a mistaken intuition about the nature of living things. In a subtle way, **the preservation of pattern and loss of substance** is a normal part of everyday life. As we humans eat and excrete, old cells within our bodies die, break up, and are expelled and replaced by copies made of fresh materials... Every atom present within us at birth is likely to have been replaced half way through our life."

**Verdict:** Moravec glosses his own use of "substance" himself, in the very next sentences, as body-stuff / atoms / metabolic turnover. "Loss of substance" = ordinary biological replacement of matter. **P.117 denies only that personhood is tied to a specific material substrate. It does not deny substance-hood as such**, and this passage is not evidence he denies any bearer, material or otherwise. Do not read "not a substance" as a general anti-substance/pure-pattern metaphysical thesis on the strength of this page alone.

Two further passages bear on the broader thesis, but are separate claims on separate pages — don't collapse them into "p.117":

- **p.120:** "The pattern-identity position has clear dualistic implications— it allows the mind to be separated from the body. Though mind is entirely the consequence of interacting matter, the ability to copy it from one storage medium to another would give it an independence and an identity apart from the machinery that runs the program." — Moravec here grants the pattern an independent *identity*, while insisting it is "entirely the consequence of interacting matter" (materialist grounding, not Cartesian substance).
- **Appendix 3** (referenced from ch.4, elsewhere in the book): "his mind is the abstract mathematical property that is shared by the old and the transformed programs... Mind, as I have defined it, is thus not only not tied to a particular body, it is not even bound to a particular pattern." This is the two-level substrate-independence claim (not tied to body *and* not tied to specific computational form) — genuinely present in the primary text, and it does name an abstract mathematical property as what the mind *is*, functioning as a kind of non-material bearer. This is a distinct, later-in-book move from the p.117 "mere jelly" passage.

### 2. "Transcendent Mind" and work identity — confirmed distinct works

"Transcendent Mind" appears in the notebook **only** as the subtitle of *Robot: Mere Machine to Transcendent Mind* (Oxford, 1999) — three independent bibliographic occurrences (Asaro's 2001 review heading; reference list of Moravec's 2003 ACM article; "More to Explore" list in Moravec's 2008 *Scientific American* article). ### 3. Fate of the body — mostly discarded, with one qualification

Two destructive scenarios, both textually clean:
- **Surgical uploading (ch.4):** brain tissue excised layer by layer and aspirated; "Your suddenly abandoned body goes into spasms and dies," followed by reconnection to "a shiny new body of the style, color, and material of your choice."
- **Matter transmitter (p.117–118):** "The transmitter scans and disassembles my jellylike body... and ends up in other jelly at the destination." Note this is a body *swap* (jelly for jelly), not a transcendence of embodiment as such.

One partial complication, but it does **not** give Moravec an "avatar/one-option-among-several" framing comparable to Uegaki's: the neutron-star "beaming" passage (p.114) says "Your original body could be kept dormant during the trip... Alternatively, the original could be kept active. Then there would be two separate versions of you." This is **copy-branching** (two divergent instances of one mind, a standard pattern-identity move), not a case where a single continuous embodiment persists as an option alongside the uploaded pattern. It softens only the claim that the body is *always* destroyed — it does not soften the Moravec/Uegaki contrast at the level that matters.

Also, flagging for accuracy since this is the one verification pass this citation gets: P563 says the pattern is "copied to a **better** substrate" — that word is the manuscript's interpolation. Nothing at p.117 claims improvement; "better" derives instead from the "shiny new body of the style, color, and material of your choice" (surgical scenario) and the neutron-star speed-up passage. Not wrong, but worth knowing it's synthesized from elsewhere in the book, not from p.117 itself.

### 4. Verdict on the Moravec/Uegaki contrast — sound only if restated

**As currently framed** ("pattern, not substance" vs. "acquires 実体"), the contrast **equivocates**: Moravec's "substance" (per his own gloss at p.117) means *matter/body-stuff*; Uegaki's 実体 renders the Aristotelian/Cartesian sense of substance-as-bearer. On a naive reading, both thinkers actually agree that matter is dispensable — Moravec just doesn't say what, if anything, "acquires" bearer-status in its place. Left unflagged this way, it reads as comparing two different senses of "substance," which is exactly the kind of imprecision 米 (Mi) is watching for.

**Restated at the correct level, the contrast is real and worth keeping:** for Moravec, what survives biological death is *only* the pattern — loss of material substance is framed as unremarkable, continuous with ordinary metabolic turnover, not a metaphysical event requiring a new kind of bearer. For Uegaki's 思念体, transcending the body is explicitly the *acquisition* of 実体 — the loss of the body produces a positive ontological gain, not a bare pattern persisting without a bearer. The opposition is therefore not "pattern vs. substance" flatly, but: *does losing the body leave you with a bearer-less pattern (Moravec) or a newly-acquired bearer (Uegaki)?*

**Suggested clause** for the 思念体 footnote (verbatim Moravec wording, level-shift made explicit rather than left to a hedge):

> Moravec's own gloss of "substance" here is material: he equates "loss of substance" with the ordinary turnover of body-stuff ("preservation of pattern and loss of substance is a normal part of everyday life," 1988: 117) — his claim is that only the *pattern* need survive, not that no bearer of any kind survives. Uegaki's 思念体, by contrast, is glossed as *acquiring* 実体 upon leaving the body — a positive ontological gain rather than a bearer-less remainder. The two therefore diverge not on whether "substance" survives in the same sense, but on whether transcending embodiment is bearer-loss (Moravec) or bearer-acquisition (Uegaki).
